The asymmetry that shapes everything

A missed failure costs a year.
A false alarm costs a conversation.

Teachers with large classes cannot track every student. Low early grades, past failures and absences usually surface only after the final exam — when it is too late to help. So this pipeline is built recall-first: find (almost) every student who will fail, and accept extra flags as the price.

Missed failure (false negative)

A student who needed help slips through and loses the year. The pipeline weights errors on the failing class 3× costlier (class_weight="balanced") and grid-search optimizes recall, never accuracy.

False alarm (false positive)

A short check-in with a student who was fine. Flagging more students than will actually fail is intentional — the decision threshold is tuned to hit 90% recall with as few unnecessary flags as possible.

Timing is the whole point

G3 is the answer and is dropped; G2 arrives too late and is dropped; G1 is kept for the mid-term watchlist. Compare both runs below to see what predicting earlier costs.

Under the hood

Eight steps, leak-free

Swipe or use the arrows — the gallery snaps one step at a time, exactly how the notebook runs.

01

Fetch

UCI zip → student-por.csv (649 × 33), handles the nested-zip mirror quirk. No manual downloads.

02

Target

fail = 1 if final grade G3 < 10 (Portuguese 0–20 scale). Sanity-check class balance and missing values.

03

Preprocess

One-hot encode categoricals, pass numerics through. Stratified 75/25 split keeps the failing class in both sets.

04

Tune for recall

5-fold stratified CV, scoring="recall" — not accuracy. Balanced class weights make misses costlier.

05

Threshold

Highest threshold on out-of-fold training probabilities that still reaches 90% recall. The test set is never peeked at.

06

Evaluate

Held-out test at 0.50 and the tuned threshold: recall, FN, TP, FP, precision, F1, accuracy + confusion matrices.

07

Explain

Random Forest feature importances → top 12 risk drivers in plain language for teachers and the report.

08

Watchlist

Risk %, priority tier, reasons. Sorted by risk; only students at or above the tuned threshold appear. Saved as CSV.

Non-negotiables

A tool for teachers, never a label for students

The watchlist supports human judgement — it must not rank, expose or penalise anyone.

Support, not punishment

Flags trigger a conversation and support — never public ranking, labelling or discipline.

Validate locally first

Data comes from two Portuguese schools. Before real use, retrain and re-check on your own students’ records.

Sensitive features

Parental education and alcohol use direct support only. They must never be used to stigmatise a child or a family.

Over-flagging is intentional

More flags than failures is the design: a false alarm costs a conversation, a missed failure costs a year.
